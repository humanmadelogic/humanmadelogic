# SEO vs AEO vs GEO: what each one means for a small business

**By Rowan Blake**
Reviewed by Kuntal Khanapurkar, Founder
Last updated: [TBC: publish date, pending the guides hub going live]

## The short answer

SEO helps your pages rank in search results. AEO (answer engine optimisation) shapes content so it can be lifted into direct answers and featured snippets. GEO (generative engine optimisation) helps AI assistants understand your brand well enough to mention it. They overlap heavily, so most small businesses should plan them as one job.

If you only remember one line from this guide, make it that last one. Three acronyms, one body of work. The rest of this page explains what each one means in practice, where they differ and what to do first if you're a founder with a small team and no patience for jargon.

## What is SEO?

SEO, search engine optimisation, is the work of helping search engines find, understand and rank your pages. It's the oldest of the three and still the foundation for the other two.

In practice it covers three things. Technical health: can search engines crawl and index your site, and does it work properly on a phone? Relevance: does each page clearly cover one topic that people actually search for? Trust: do other sites mention and link to you, and does your content show real expertise?

Google's own guidance is a useful sense check. It says its ranking systems are designed to prioritise helpful, reliable information created to benefit people, rather than content made to manipulate rankings ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). That's a good rule for all three acronyms, not just SEO.

## What is AEO (answer engine optimisation)?

AEO, answer engine optimisation, is about being the answer, not just a link. It means writing pages that open with a clear, direct reply to a real question, structuring them with helpful headings and lists and marking them up so search engines and assistants can pick out the answer confidently.

Think of the boxes that sit above or beside the normal results: featured snippets, "People also ask" panels and spoken answers from voice assistants. Those formats favour pages that get to the point. If someone asks "how long does a rebrand take?", a page that opens with a straight answer gives a search engine something easy to lift. A page that opens with three paragraphs about the company's history doesn't.

The format is simple: question, direct answer, then detail. A skincare brand might answer "can I use this with retinol?" in its first two sentences, then explain. A clinic might answer "do I need a referral?". Same shape, different questions.

## What is GEO (generative engine optimisation)?

GEO stands for generative engine optimisation. It's the work of making your brand easy for AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini to understand, trust and name. It builds on SEO basics, then adds consistent brand facts across the web and content that answers questions in plain, quotable language.

When someone asks an AI assistant "who are the best indie skincare brands in the UK?", the assistant builds an answer from what it can find and what it has learned. Exactly how each tool picks its sources isn't fully public, so treat anyone who claims to know the formula with caution. What's reasonable to say is that a brand described clearly and consistently, on its own site and on other sites, is easier for any system to understand and repeat.

Some of the practical side is documented. OpenAI and Perplexity both publish guidance on the crawlers their search features use and how sites can allow them ([OpenAI](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots), [Perplexity](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots)). Our guide to getting cited by ChatGPT covers that step by step.

## How they overlap, and why you plan them together

A small business usually needs all three, but not as three projects. One plan covers them: fix the technical basics, build pages around the questions buyers ask, open each with a direct answer and make your brand facts consistent everywhere. That single body of work serves search rankings, featured answers and AI mentions at the same time.

The overlap is bigger than the acronyms suggest. The same page can rank in search results, be lifted into a featured answer and be cited by an AI assistant. Google says the same SEO best practices apply to its AI features, such as AI Overviews and AI Mode. It also says there are no additional requirements or special optimisations needed to appear in them ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)).

| | SEO | AEO | GEO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rank your pages in search results | Be the direct answer to a question | Be understood and named by AI assistants |
| Where you show up | Search results pages | Featured snippets, answer panels and voice answers | Answers in tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI features |
| What helps | Technical health, relevant pages, links and mentions | Answer-first pages, clear headings and structured data that matches the page | Consistent brand facts, quotable answers and mentions on trusted sites |
| How you check | Search Console and your rankings for key searches | Whether your answer appears for your key questions | Asking assistants your buyers' questions and logging who gets named |

That's why we don't sell them as three separate jobs. Splitting them usually means three sets of reports and one confused website. One plan, done well, serves all three.

## What a small business should do first

If you're starting from scratch, here's a sensible order. None of it needs a big budget. It needs focus.

1. **Fix the technical basics.** Make sure search engines can crawl your site, your pages work properly on a phone and nothing important is hidden in images or behind a login.
2. **List the questions your buyers ask.** Pull them from sales calls, emails, reviews, DMs and search suggestions. These become your pages and FAQs.
3. **Answer first.** Open each page with a direct answer in a sentence or two, then add the detail. Use headings that match the questions.
4. **Make your brand facts consistent.** Your name, what you do, who it's for and where you work should read the same on your site, your LinkedIn page and any directory that lists you.
5. **Check, log and repeat.** Once a month, search your key questions and ask a few AI assistants the same ones, then fix the gaps you find. Our guide to [getting your brand cited by ChatGPT](/guides/how-to-get-your-brand-cited-by-chatgpt) has a blank tracking log you can copy.

## Common myths, answered calmly

**"You need a separate GEO agency."** You don't. GEO builds on the same foundations as SEO. A separate supplier for each acronym tends to create overlap and gaps, not clarity.

**"More pages means more chances."** Google's people-first guidance lists producing lots of content in the hope that some of it performs well as a warning sign ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). Fewer, better pages that properly answer real questions are a stronger bet.

The other myths you'll hear, such as "SEO is dead", "you need special AI files" and "you can pay to be the answer", are answered in the questions below.

## Questions people ask about SEO, AEO and GEO

**Is SEO dead now that people use AI?**
No. AI answers still need sources, and Google says the same SEO best practices apply to its AI Overviews and AI Mode, with no special extra optimisation required. Pages that are easy to crawl, clear and genuinely helpful give search engines and AI assistants something worth showing.

**Is GEO the same as geotargeting?**
No. In older SEO talk, geo usually meant geotargeting: showing the right pages or ads to people in a particular place. GEO in the AI sense means generative engine optimisation, making your brand easy for AI assistants to understand and mention. Same letters, different jobs. Context usually tells you which.

**How long do SEO, AEO and GEO take to work?**
Longer than ads, and nobody can give you an honest fixed date. Technical fixes can land early. Rankings and AI mentions build more slowly, as search engines recrawl your site and learn to trust your content. Because all three rest on the same foundations, progress on one usually helps the others.

**Can anyone guarantee AI mentions?**
No. Nobody controls what AI assistants say, just as nobody controls Google's results. Be wary of anyone selling guaranteed placement. What you can control is how clearly your brand is described on your own site and elsewhere. You can also control how directly your pages answer the questions people actually ask.

**Do I need llms.txt?**
Probably not yet. llms.txt is an optional file some sites add to point AI tools at their key pages. It's cheap to add, but it isn't a confirmed ranking signal. Google says you don't need AI text files to appear in its AI features. Get the basics right first.
