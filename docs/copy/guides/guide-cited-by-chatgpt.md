# How to get your brand cited by ChatGPT (and other AI assistants)

**By Rowan Blake**
Reviewed by Kuntal Khanapurkar, Founder
Last updated: [TBC: publish date, pending the guides hub going live]

## The short answer

Make your brand easy to understand and easy to verify. Keep the same clear facts about who you are everywhere, publish pages that answer real buyer questions directly, allow AI search crawlers to read your site and earn mentions on sites AI tools already trust. Then check what assistants say every month.

> **Nobody can guarantee that ChatGPT, or any other AI assistant, will cite your brand. Not us, not an agency, not a tool. Nobody controls what assistants say, so anyone selling a guaranteed citation is selling something they can't deliver.**

What you can do is give these tools every reason to understand you, trust you and repeat you. That's what the six steps below are for. None of them are tricks. They're the same fundamentals that make a brand easy for a person to understand, applied to a new kind of reader.

## How ChatGPT finds and chooses sources

It draws on pages its search crawler is allowed to read. OpenAI says sites that opt out of its OAI-SearchBot crawler won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers, apart from plain navigational links. It doesn't publish how sources are then chosen, so clear, direct pages that other sites back up are your best bet.

OpenAI also separates search from training. Its documentation describes OAI-SearchBot as the crawler for search and GPTBot as the crawler for content that may be used to train its models, and it says each setting is independent ([OpenAI](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots)). So you can appear in ChatGPT search while opting out of training.

## Step 1: Check what AI says about you today

Ask it the questions your customers would ask, such as "best skincare brands for sensitive skin in the UK" or "who can help a clinic get more reviews". Record whether you're named and which sources it cites. Repeat monthly with the same prompts in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI results.

Start with a short list of prompts and keep the wording fixed, so you're comparing like with like. For example:

- "What are the best [your category] brands in [your country]?"
- "Who can help with [the problem you solve] in [your town or region]?"
- "Is [your brand] any good?"
- "What's the difference between [your brand] and [a competitor]?"
- "Which [category] brands are good for [a specific need]?"

Answers can change between sessions, so one answer tells you very little. A pattern over a few months tells you a lot.

## Step 2: Make your brand facts identical everywhere

AI tools piece together who you are from lots of places. If your site calls you "skincare for sensitive skin", your LinkedIn page says "skincare for everyone" and a directory still lists your old name, you're making that job harder.

Write a short, plain description of your brand: your name, what you make or do, who it's for and where you sell. Use it, word for word or close to it, on your homepage, your About page, your LinkedIn page and any directory or marketplace profile you control. If your site has structured data about your organisation, make sure it matches what's visible on the page. Google lists matching structured data to visible text among its best practices for its AI features ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)).

## Step 3: Publish pages that answer real questions, answer first

Assistants answer questions, so give them answers. Collect the questions your buyers actually ask: in DMs, emails, reviews, sales calls and search suggestions. Turn the important ones into pages or FAQ sections.

Open each one with a direct answer in a sentence or two, then add the detail. Keep facts specific and checkable. "Ships to the UK and Ireland" is easier to repeat than "ships to lots of places". Only say what's true and what you can stand behind, especially in a category like skincare, where claims have rules.

This is the same answer-first habit that helps with featured snippets in search. Our guide to [SEO, AEO and GEO](/guides/seo-vs-aeo-vs-geo) explains how the three overlap.

## Step 4: Let the right crawlers in

Check your robots.txt file (yoursite.com/robots.txt). If it blocks the crawlers AI tools use for search, those tools may not be able to show your pages in their results. OpenAI recommends allowing OAI-SearchBot and Perplexity recommends allowing PerplexityBot if you want to appear in their results ([OpenAI](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots), [Perplexity](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots)).

A simple example:

```
User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /
```

The GPTBot lines are optional. They only cover training, so leave them out if you're happy for your content to be used that way. Some hosting and security tools block bots by default, so check those settings too. OpenAI says it can take around 24 hours for its search systems to adjust after a robots.txt change ([OpenAI](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots)), and Perplexity says changes may take up to 24 hours to be reflected ([Perplexity](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots)).

For Google's AI features, Google says robots.txt rules for Googlebot are the control, and a page must be indexed and eligible to show in Google Search with a snippet ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). If your pages already show in Google Search with a snippet, there's no extra technical step for its AI features.

## Step 5: Earn mentions on sites AI tools already trust

Your own site is one voice. AI tools tend to look at what others say too. How much weight each tool gives to other sites isn't public, so treat this as good practice rather than a formula.

Good places to start: genuinely useful industry directories, editorial roundups and gift guides in your category, podcasts and newsletters your buyers read and review platforms where real customers leave real reviews. For a skincare brand that might mean beauty editors' roundups. For a clinic, it's often local directories and Google reviews.

Skip the shortcuts. Paid link schemes, fake reviews and spammy "best of" lists can do more harm than good. Google's review policy prohibits offering incentives for reviews and asking only happy customers ([Google](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)).

## Step 6: Track it monthly

Keep a simple log. Same prompts, same tools, once a month. Here's a blank template to copy:

| Date | Tool | Prompt | Named? (yes or no) | Sources cited | What to fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [date] | ChatGPT | [your prompt] | [your result] | [sources it cites] | [your notes] |
| [date] | Perplexity | [same prompt] | [your result] | [sources it cites] | [your notes] |
| [date] | Gemini | [same prompt] | [your result] | [sources it cites] | [your notes] |

Over a few months the log shows which sources keep coming up, which becomes your shortlist for step 5. It also shows whether your changes are moving things, without reading too much into any single answer.

## What doesn't work

- **Buying "guaranteed" placement.** Nobody controls what assistants say.
- **Fake or paid reviews.** They break Google's review policy and wreck trust when they're spotted.
- **Keyword stuffing.** Pages written for robots read badly for people. Google's guidance warns against content made mainly to attract search engines.
- **Vague copy.** If a person can't tell what you do at a glance, a machine will struggle too.

## Can I pay to be listed in ChatGPT?

Not as a citation. OpenAI's crawler documentation lists OAI-AdsBot, which checks the safety of pages submitted as ads on ChatGPT ([OpenAI](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots)). But an ad is a paid placement, not your brand being cited in an answer, and nobody can sell you a guaranteed citation. Check OpenAI's current ads information before you spend anything.

## Questions about getting cited by AI

**How long does it take to get mentioned by AI assistants?**
There's no reliable timescale, and anyone who quotes one is guessing. Changes to your site have to be crawled again, and mentions on other sites take time to earn. Set checkpoints, run the same prompts each month and judge progress by the trend over several months, not by a single answer.

**Does ChatGPT use Google or Bing to find sources?**
OpenAI's crawler documentation doesn't say, so we won't guess. What it does say is that ChatGPT search shows sites its OAI-SearchBot crawler is allowed to read. So allow that crawler, open your pages with clear answers and earn genuine mentions elsewhere. Those steps are worth doing whichever index sits behind it.

**Should I block AI crawlers?**
Only if you're happy to drop out of those tools' search results. OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers. Perplexity recommends allowing PerplexityBot to appear in its results. Training is a separate choice: you can block OpenAI's GPTBot on its own.

**Do reviews matter for AI answers?**
They can help. Genuine reviews on trusted platforms are one way other sites back up what you say about yourself, which may make your brand easier to trust. Nobody outside these companies knows exactly how much they count. Ask real customers, never pay for reviews and follow Google's review policy.

**Is this different for Perplexity?**
The basics are the same. Perplexity says PerplexityBot is designed to surface and link websites in its search results, and it recommends allowing it in your robots.txt. So the same steps apply: clear facts, answer-first pages, open access for its crawler and genuine mentions elsewhere.
