# Google's November 2026 core update and Gemini 4 Hydrogen: what founders should do

By Rowan Blake
Reviewed by Kuntal Khanapurkar, Founder
Published: [TBC: publish date]

**The short answer.** Google started its [November 2026 core update](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/WLxcJyC6jMWMfryxsKMc) on 10 November and put Gemini 4 Hydrogen live across Search on [12 November](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-4-hydrogen-search-upgrade/). If your rankings or AI citations move this month, two changes overlap. Log both dates, wait for the rollout to finish and then compare like with like.

## What changed in the core update?

Google's [Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/WLxcJyC6jMWMfryxsKMc) says it "Released the November 2026 core update, which applies globally and to all languages. The rollout may take up to 2 weeks to complete." The entry is dated 10 November. When we checked on 18 November, the dashboard did not yet show the rollout as complete.

[Search Engine Journal reports](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/seo-pulse-core-update-chatgpt-business-hub-ai-modes-new-model/597135/) that it is the third Search core update of the year, and that Google's Search Central team called it "a regular update".

So this isn't a UK-only wobble. Founders in Australia, Canada and Singapore are working with the same update.

## What is Gemini 4 Hydrogen?

It's Google's new model for Search. Google calls it "the biggest model upgrade to Search yet" and says "Hydrogen is now available globally in Google Search for all users" ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-4-hydrogen-search-upgrade/)).

[Search Engine Journal reports](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/seo-pulse-core-update-chatgpt-business-hub-ai-modes-new-model/597135/) that Hydrogen became the default model in AI Mode on 12 November, and that Robby Stein, VP of Product at Google Search, said in a thread on X that it is "being used for your hardest questions in AI Overviews".

Two lines from [Google's announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-4-hydrogen-search-upgrade/) matter most for brands:

- "Hydrogen's advanced reasoning supercharges our query fan-out technique in Search, connecting you to a broader range of helpful links than before."
- "You'll see more images, structured responses, comparison tables, and custom tools built for you."

[Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-4-hydrogen-search-upgrade/) also says Search now runs "simultaneous queries across all the relevant subtopics" of a question. In plain English, one question from a buyer can become several smaller searches behind the scenes, and each one is a chance for your page to be picked up or passed over.

One caution. [SEJ notes](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/seo-pulse-core-update-chatgpt-business-hub-ai-modes-new-model/597135/) that "The Search post and the thread do not give link numbers." So "a broader range of helpful links" is Google's claim, not a measured figure.

## Why the timing makes your data harder to read

Two changes landed two days apart, so a dip or a jump in November could come from either. [SEJ's advice](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/seo-pulse-core-update-chatgpt-business-hub-ai-modes-new-model/597135/) is to log both dates, so you can compare what happened before and after each one.

There's a seasonal catch too. [SEJ reports](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/seo-pulse-core-update-chatgpt-business-hub-ai-modes-new-model/597135/) that Google advises waiting at least a full week after a core update has finished, then comparing that week in Search Console with the week before the rollout began. On Google's two-week estimate, SEJ points out, that first full week would include US Thanksgiving, Black Friday and Cyber Monday. If you sell online, you could end up comparing holiday traffic with an ordinary week and reading the difference as the update.

To our eye, the worst move this month is a panicked rewrite of your whole site while the data is still muddy.

## Is Google sending more people to websites from AI answers?

Google says it is adding more links, but neither of its posts gives click numbers. On 15 October, [Google said](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/updates-ai-mode-overviews-explore-web/): "We've made continued and substantial increases to the presence of inline links in our AI experiences." The same post says people want more choice over sources: "the number of people who now use our Preferred Sources feature has grown by more than 7x since the start of the year." That counts people choosing sources, not clicks, and Google doesn't say which markets it covers.

We think the honest reading is this: more links are on offer, but nobody outside Google can yet say how many clicks they send. Your own Search Console and analytics are the only numbers that count for your brand.

## What this means for your brand

- **Log first, act later.** Note 10 and 12 November in your reporting, then wait for Google's dashboard to show the update as complete before you judge anything.
- **Compare like with like.** If your trade is seasonal, set the post-update week against a similar week, not against Black Friday.
- **Answer the whole question.** Because Search now fans out across subtopics, pages that clearly answer each part of a buyer's question (price, fit, delivery, aftercare) give Google more to link to. That's our view, based on how Google describes fan-out.
- **Check your AI answers by hand.** Ask the same buyer questions in AI Mode and AI Overviews before and after, with the same wording, and note whether your brand is linked or named.
- **Keep one set of facts.** Make your name, what you do and where you sell read the same on your site, LinkedIn and anywhere else you're listed, so any model has a clear picture to work from. This one is our view, not something Google said this month.
