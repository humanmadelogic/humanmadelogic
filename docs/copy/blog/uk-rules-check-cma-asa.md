**The short answer.** UK regulators have said plainly what they're watching. The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has [opened 20 investigations](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/10/14/direct-consumer-enforcement-18-months-on/) under its direct enforcement powers, and the Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) has [upheld a ruling against a "Carbon Neutral Delivery" search ad](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/procook-ltd.html) found partly with AI monitoring. If you sell to UK consumers, prices, reviews and green claims are in focus.

This post is UK only: it covers UK regulators and UK rules, so don't assume the same applies in Australia, Canada or Singapore. It's a summary of what the regulators have published, not legal advice. If any of it touches your business, check with a qualified adviser.

## What has the CMA done with its new powers?

On 14 October 2026 the CMA published [an 18-month update](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/10/14/direct-consumer-enforcement-18-months-on/) on its direct consumer enforcement regime under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCC Act).

Since April 2026, the CMA says it has opened 6 new investigations, "taking the total number of investigations we have opened to 20". It has also "ordered redress of over £2.3 million and imposed fines of over £11.8 million" ([CMA](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/10/14/direct-consumer-enforcement-18-months-on/)).

To our eye, the useful part for a small brand is not who is being investigated but what the CMA says it expects from every business.

## What does the CMA expect on prices and discounts?

The CMA's expectation is written in one line: "Discounts must be genuine and businesses must show the total price clearly and throughout the purchasing journey, without opting customers in by pre-selecting extra services." ([CMA](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/10/14/direct-consumer-enforcement-18-months-on/))

It names drip pricing, partition pricing and misleading time limited offers as continuing priorities, and says it is reviewing how online retailers disclose delivery fees. We think that puts the checkout, the delivery line and any "was" price squarely in view for a DTC brand.

## What does the CMA say about fake reviews, including staff ones?

Fake and misleading reviews are the second big theme. The CMA has [opened investigations into how several businesses handle reviews](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/10/14/direct-consumer-enforcement-18-months-on/), over concerns that "consumer law on misleading reviews may have been broken". Those are open investigations, not findings. The CMA's wording is worth reading slowly: "commissioning reviews can take many forms and isn't limited to paying consumers for them, for example unlawful conduct could include a business requesting members of staff to write reviews about its products or services." ([CMA](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/10/14/direct-consumer-enforcement-18-months-on/))

So, in our view, asking your team for a quick five-star review is not a harmless launch tactic. The CMA also says businesses that host or use reviews should have effective systems to prevent misleading content.

## What changes for subscriptions in January?

If you sell refills, boxes or memberships, note this: "A new subscription contracts regime will come into force in January to ensure customers are better informed and make it easier for people to cancel plans they no longer want." ([CMA](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/10/14/direct-consumer-enforcement-18-months-on/))

The CMA says the government will publish secondary legislation and further guidance ahead of it, and that its early focus will be helping businesses comply. It also warns that businesses "should not assume that our support for compliance means enforcement of the rules is being delayed."

## Does using AI change your responsibilities?

The CMA says it is monitoring how AI products are marketed and used. Its expectation is short: "New technology does not change their responsibility to comply with consumer law." ([CMA](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2026/10/14/direct-consumer-enforcement-18-months-on/)) We read that simply: using an AI tool for product copy or customer chat doesn't hand the responsibility to the tool.

## What did the ASA rule on "carbon neutral delivery"?

On 11 November 2026 the ASA published [a ruling on Procook Ltd](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/procook-ltd.html), a kitchenware retailer. It is a public ruling and we name the company only as the ASA does. A paid Google search ad said "Carbon Neutral Delivery" without saying whether that relied on offsetting or on actually cutting emissions.

The ASA says the ruling "forms part of a wider piece of work on ads making carbon neutral delivery or similar claims", and that the ads were found partly through "the Active Ad Monitoring system, which uses AI to proactively search for online ads that might break the rules." ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/procook-ltd.html))

The detail that matters for every small advertiser is about space. The ad "had a limit of 270 characters, therefore, it was not so limited that ProCook could not have made the basis of the claim clear". The landing page didn't explain it either. The ASA concluded the ad was "likely to mislead" and that it breached CAP Code (Edition 12) rules 3.1, 3.3 (Misleading advertising) and 11.1 (Environmental claims) ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/procook-ltd.html)).

Our takeaway: a short ad is not an excuse, and the ASA says software helped find this one.

## What does this mean for your brand?

We think the clearest brand is also the safest one. Five checks we'd run, based on what the regulators have published:

1. **Show the total price early.** Walk your own checkout and note where delivery and extras first appear. Nothing should be pre-ticked.
2. **Keep discounts genuine.** If a "was" price or countdown isn't real, cut it.
3. **Clean up how you get reviews.** No staff reviews, no paid ones and a simple way to spot and act on misleading content.
4. **Put the basis of a green claim in the ad itself.** If you say carbon neutral, say how, even in a short format.
5. **Read your subscription journey before January.** Can someone see what they're signing up for and cancel easily? Watch for the government's guidance.
